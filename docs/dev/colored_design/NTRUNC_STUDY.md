# N-truncation of the colored scan: which B_p die in the gl_N theory

Scope: 3-colored gl_6 (the user's narrowed target). Scratch crate: `ntrunc/t1` (path dependency on krhom; no repository edits). Raw outputs: `ntrunc/t1/out/*.txt`. Scripts: `ntrunc/scripts/` (`greene.py`, `agg.py`, `pdf2txt.py`). Papers (downloaded and text-extracted): `ntrunc/papers/`.

## 0. Result in one paragraph

**Rule (verified).** Fix N. A type p is a contingency table with row sums lambda and column sums mu. Its indecomposable B_p maps to zero in the gl_N theory iff mu(p)_1 > N. Here mu(p) is the RSK shape of p with its columns reversed, which equals the transposed Robinson-Schensted shape of the longest double-coset element p_max. By Greene's theorem the rule needs no RSK:

    mu(p)_1 = the largest sum of table entries along a lattice path that moves down or left (row index weakly increasing, column index weakly decreasing).

So p is killed iff some such down-left chain of entries adds up to more than N. This is an O(nm) dynamic program.

**Checks.**
- The rule agrees with an independent trace criterion on all 9 833 (type, N) pairs: 51 (lambda, N) rows, every reachable mu, N from max(lambda) to K. The one exception is (3,3,3) at N = 9 = K, which is cloud-only; there the rule kills nothing.
- It also agrees with the transposed RS shape of p_max, with the diagonal pairing <b_p, b_p>_N, and with the max label of the minimal-flow ladder (all zero mismatches).
- The Greene formula agrees with RSK on 19 205 tables.

**Consequences.**
- Truncating at every stage is exact. The chi of the truncated K_0 class equals the untruncated chi for 463 (knot, N) checks with N <= 6.
- At N = 6 this halves the 3-strand Grothendieck lower bound (median ratio 0.49) and cuts the peak by about 2.8x (median 0.36). Surviving types: (3,3,3): 40 of 55; (3,3,3,3): 790 of 2 008.
- **The Gram size is controlled by the Gr(3,6)^4 box, not by the truncation:** for (3,3,3,3) at N = 6 a Gram entry has at most 13 656 unknowns (777 reduced), against 1.9e9 unboxed.
- A canonical-basis search limited to labels <= N finds every surviving type. It reproduces b_p exactly on (2,2,2) and (3,3,3). On (3,3,3,3) it does not, because peeling needs killed b_q that have no ladder within the bound. The fix is in §4.

## 1. Literature

### 1(a) Decategorified: the kernel is spanned by canonical basis elements of cells with a part > N

1. **CKM**: Cautis-Kamnitzer-Morrison, *Webs and quantum skew Howe duality*, arXiv:1210.6437 (Math. Ann. 360, 2014).
   - **Thm 4.2.2(3)**: ∧_q(C^N ⊗ C^m) = ⊕_μ V(μ^t) ⊗ V(μ) as U_q(gl_m) ⊗ U_q(sl_N)-modules, where μ runs over the N-bounded gl_m weights (μ_1 <= N).
   - **Lemma 4.4.2**: U̇/I_λ ≅ ⊕_{μ<=λ} End V(μ), where I_λ is the ideal generated by the 1_ν that λ does not dominate. They credit [Doty03, Thm 4.2].
   - **Thm 4.4.1** (and §1, eq. (1.2)): Φ^N_m is fully faithful. Equivalently, the kernel of U̇(gl_m) → ⊕_K End_{sl_N} ∧^K(C^N ⊗ C^m) is the ideal generated by the 1_k with k not N-bounded.
   - **Remark after Thm 4.4.1**: the quotients U̇/I_{λ(K)} are Doty's generalized q-Schur algebras.
2. **Doty**, *Presenting generalized q-Schur algebras*, math/0305208.
   - **Thm 2.4** = Lusztig, *Introduction to quantum groups*, **29.3.3** (refined Peter-Weyl): U̇[>=λ] and U̇[>λ] are two-sided ideals "generated as vector spaces by their intersections with Ḃ".
   - **§4.1** = Lusztig **29.2**: for a cofinite coideal P of X^+, U̇[P] = span ⊔_{λ∈P} Ḃ[λ] is a two-sided ideal, and the quotient is semisimple with dimension Σ_{λ∉P} (dim Λ_λ)^2.
   - **Thm 4.2**: S(π) ≅ U̇/U̇[P].
   - **Cor 4.3, §4.4**: the canonical basis of S(π) is the set of nonzero images of Ḃ, namely the union of the two-sided cells Ḃ[λ] with λ ∈ π.
   - **§2.5**: the Ḃ[λ] are the two-sided cells of Lusztig 29.4.1.
3. **Assembly.** P_N = {ν dominant : ν_1 > N} is a coideal for dominance, since ν' ⊵ ν implies ν'_1 >= ν_1. So the kernel of S(m,K) on ∧^K(C^m ⊗ C^N) is span{b : cell(b) = ν, ν_1 > N}.
   - CKM and TVW (arXiv:1504.05069, Introduction: "Φ^m_skew is full and its kernel is generated by killing gl_m-weights with entries not in {0..N}") give "kernel = ideal of non-N-bounded idempotents".
   - Lusztig 29.2 gives "that ideal = span of the canonical basis in the cells with ν_1 > N".
4. **Cells as tables.** Cui-Luo-Wang, arXiv:2004.00193.
   - **Prop 5.6(3)** (affine version of [Du96 = J. Du, *Cells in certain sets of matrices*, Tohoku Math. J. 48 (1996)], Lemma 2.2): C ~_LR C' iff w^+_C ~_LR w^+_{C'}.
   - So the two-sided cell of b_p is the KL two-sided cell of p_max in S_K. In type A that cell is given by the Robinson-Schensted shape (KL79; Lusztig). This explains mu(p) = RS(p_max)^T.
   - Background: Du, *q-Schur algebras, asymptotic forms, and quantum SL_n*, J. Algebra 177 (1995); BLM 1990.
5. **Identification [?].** krhom's b_p is Williamson's singular KL basis. That it equals the image of Lusztig's U̇ canonical basis (BLM / Du normalization) is assumed (R1 §1.4 already flags it). The exact agreement in §2 is strong evidence in our normalization.
6. **Kernel as an ideal.** MSV, arXiv:1008.1348, §1 and §2 (citing Doty-Giaquinto [7] 2.4): the kernel of U̇(gl_n) → S_q(n,d) is the ideal generated by the 1_λ with λ ∉ Λ(n,d).

### 1(b) Categorified

**I found no paper that states "Foam_N kills exactly the indecomposables B_p whose classes lie in the skew Howe kernel".** The closest statements:

1. **QR**: Queffelec-Rose, *The sl_n foam 2-category*, arXiv:1405.5920.
   - **Def 2.4**: Ǔ_Q(gl_m)^(n) is the n-bounded quotient. It kills the identity 2-morphisms of 1_a with some a_i < 0 or a_i > n, and is taken inside the divided-power part of the Karoubi envelope.
   - **Prop 3.22**: nFoam(N) ≃ the direct limit of N·Ǔ_Q(gl_m)^(n).
   - **Remark 3.24**: ∞Foam(N) ≃ N·Ǔ_Q(gl_∞)^{>=0}, the categorified q-Schur algebras.
   - So Foam_N is the categorified idempotent quotient. This is the 2-categorical form of the CKM kernel statement.
2. **LQR**, arXiv:1212.6076: the N = 2 case, and the Karoubi / K_0 discussion in §2.1.5.
3. **MSV**, arXiv:1008.1348: S(n,d) is categorified as the quotient of U(gl_n) by the 1_λ with λ ∉ Λ(n,d). §7 treats the Grothendieck algebra and the analogous vanishing E^(m)_{±i} 1_λ = 0 iff m > λ_{i(+1)}.
4. **MY**: Mackaay-Yonezawa, arXiv:1306.6242 (matrix factorizations).
   - **Lemma 9.5**: the Euler form dim_q HOM equals the web form, so K_0 injects into webs.
   - **Cor 9.8**: K_0(Kar W°) ≅ W_Λ.
   - Thm 9.7, Cor 4.11.
   - This is exactly the "Hom formula ⇒ K_0 = webs" mechanism used below.
5. **Webster**, arXiv:1209.0051.
   - **Thm A** (Thms 8.7, 8.10): the indecomposable 1-morphisms of the categorified U̇ give Lusztig's canonical basis (simply laced).
   - **Lemma 1.15**: a full, essentially surjective functor sends each orthodox (indecomposable) basis vector to an orthodox one or to 0.
6. **KRW**: Khovanov-Robert-Wagner, *A foamy approach to Soergel bimodules*, arXiv:2603.22957.
   - **Thm 4.6**: Rep: blFoam → SSoe is an equivalence of 2-categories. Hom spaces are taken up to ∞-equivalence, so this is N-independent and not Karoubi-completed.
   - **Lemma 4.7** reduces full faithfulness to concentric circles via the QR algorithm (Prop 2.11 = [QR18, Lemma 5.2]).
7. **ETW**, arXiv:1703.06691: works with QR's foams (Prop 2.20) and §3.2 "Karoubi envelope technology". Nothing on killing indecomposables.

**Best argument (the one the engine needs), with assumptions listed.** Let Φ_N : SSBim → Kar(Foam_N) be the gl_N quotient (universal construction), as in the engine. Assume:

- (A1) **Hom formula at level N.** For ladders Γ0, Γ1: grdim Hom_{Foam_N}(Φ_N Γ0, Φ_N Γ1) = MOY_N(closure of Γ0^† Γ1), up to the standard shift. This is the universal-construction / Robert-Wagner state-space theorem, and it is the closure contract the engine already uses.
- (A2) Soergel-Williamson: [BS(L)] = Σ m_q b_q with B_q multiplicities m_q ∈ N[v^±] (characteristic 0, Elias-Williamson).
- (A3) Generic q.

Then:

1. **The pairing computes End.** By additivity and Krull-Schmidt, (A1) extends from ladders to the B_p, by induction along the unitriangular ladder → B_p system:

       grdim Hom(Φ_N B_p, Φ_N B_q) = <b_p, b_q>_N := tr_N(ω(b_p) b_q),

   with ω the transpose anti-involution.

   Hence **Φ_N(B_p) = 0 iff <b_p, b_p>_N = 0**, because an object is zero iff its End is zero.
2. **(⇐)** If b_p acts by zero, then ω(b_p) b_p does too, so <b_p, b_p>_N = 0.
3. **(⇒)** If Φ_N(B_p) = 0, then tr_N(ω(b_p) y) = 0 for every y. The trace form is nondegenerate on the semisimple image ⊕_{ν_1<=N} End V_ν: it is Σ_ν qdim V_{ν^t}(gl_N) · tr_{V_ν}, and these quantum dimensions are nonzero for generic q. So ω(b_p) is in the kernel. ω preserves cells (ι(b^λ_{S,T}) = b^λ_{T,S}, Doty §2.6), so b_p is in the kernel.
4. With §1(a): **Φ_N(B_p) = 0 iff ν(p)_1 > N.**
5. **Fullness.** Foam_N 2-morphisms are a quotient of ∞-foams, and ∞-foams = SSBim 2-morphisms by KRW Thm 4.6. So Φ_N is full. End(Φ_N B_p) is then a quotient of a graded local ring, so Φ_N(B_p) is indecomposable or 0 (Webster Lemma 1.15 argument).
6. **Second route for (⇐)**, independent of (A1): QR Prop 3.22 together with Webster Thm A, plus categorified cell positivity (b ∈ Ḃ[ν] ⇒ B_p is a summand of X ∘ 1_ν ∘ Y). This route is not checked here.

**Consequence for the scan.** Φ_N is additive, so the image of the tangle complex has zero objects exactly at the killed types. Dropping them at every stage commutes with the crossings, because the killed span is a two-sided ideal. The `crossing_cx(.., n)` pruning of labels > N is a special case: 1_μ with μ_i > N has mu_1 >= μ_i.

## 2. mu(p) and the agreement table

**Definitions** (`t1 kill`):
- **trace criterion.** b_p (block λ → μ) is killed iff tr_N(b_p L_s) = 0 for every minimal-flow ladder L_s of the opposite block (μ → λ). Equivalently, tr_N(b_p b_y) = 0 for every b_y, because the change of basis is unitriangular.
- **How the trace is computed.** tr_N(b_p L_s) = Σ_r m_r tr_N(b_r), from expand(b_p L_s) in (λ, λ). Here tr_N(b_r) is a copy of `schur::trace_b`, which evaluates `moy::closure_value` with value 0 when a level exceeds N.
- **MOY composite check.** Σ_{r,s'} c_pr closure_N(L_r ∘ L_s), with b_p written as a ladder combination, is compared with the trace above. It agreed everywhere it was run (all rows marked checked).
- **Diagonal criterion.** <b_p, b_p>_N = tr_N(b_p b_{p^T}).
- **naive.** The minimal-flow ladder has a label > N.
- **Positivity.** Every tr_N(b_r) has nonnegative coefficients (0 exceptions), which is consistent with (A1).

## 4. Truncated canonical bases

`t1 rbfs`: the schur.rs BFS (clean states, deduplicated on leading tables) with every label <= N, exhaustive. Every table it reaches is peeled (Soergel's algorithm) and compared with `Schur::canonical_basis`.

**Theory: why every surviving type has a ladder with labels <= N.** A clean ladder L (leading term h_p with coefficient 1) contains B_p as a summand. If L passes a label > N, then Φ_N(BS(L)) = 0, so B_p is killed. Hence for surviving p, *every* clean ladder, and in particular schur.rs's own BFS ladder, stays <= N. The restricted BFS therefore reaches every surviving type.

- **"Peel would need a killed b_q".** This is the number of surviving types whose peeling fails if only surviving b's are kept. So killed b_q do occur as summands of ladders of surviving types, and the restricted BFS must keep the killed tables it reaches.
- **Ladder characters can replace killed b_q.** Peeling with X_q = char(L_q) in place of b_q for killed q gives exactly b_p in all (2,2,2) and (3,3,3) cases (0 differences).
  - Reason: Soergel's algorithm works with any bar-invariant element that has leading term h_q.
  - This does **not** carry over to expanding products modulo the killed ideal. There X_q must also be ≡ b_q modulo the ideal, i.e. come from a ladder through a label > N; the minimal-flow ladder qualifies.
- **The restricted basis drives the scan exactly** on (3,3,3): `t1 rscan`, N = 6, 7, 8 on 4_1, 8_19 and 12a_1248 gives the same K_0 class and statistics as the full-basis truncated scan.

**(3,3,3,3)** (target block only; `LEAN=1` keeps only ladders in the queue):

| BFS bound | survival threshold N | states popped | time | peak RSS | surviving found | surviving peeled | missing killed b_q |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 4 | 14 189 | 5 s | 165 MB (392 MB non-lean) | 24/24 | 22 | 2 |
| 5 | 5 | 68 573 | 38 s | 561 MB | 227/227 | 211 | 12 (+4 cascading) |
| 5 | 4 (two-tier) | 68 573 | 38 s | 567 MB | 24/24 | 24/24 | 0 |

- **So for 4 strands, the BFS restricted to labels <= N does not reproduce every surviving b_p.** Some killed b_q needed by peeling have no clean ladder within the bound. A bound of N+1 fixed N = 4.
- **The truncated Conway scan at N = 4 with the bound-5 basis still fails.** Expanding products needs killed b_q that are missing; it panicked in `rexpand`.
- **Cost.** The labels <= 6 region contains 137 984 of the 160 000 tables of all blocks, so the restriction saves little for (3,3,3,3). The real saving is 790 of 2 008 types in the scan.
- **Extrapolation.** The N = 6 restricted BFS is predicted at more than 1 GB (68 573 states / 561 MB at N = 5, growing about 4x per step), so it was not run. The cloud command is in §5.
- **Recommended design** (not implemented):
  - restricted BFS for the surviving b_p;
  - for each killed table q that appears, X_q := char of its minimal-flow ladder, generated on demand (its max label is mu_1(q) > N, so X_q ≡ b_q modulo the ideal), used both in peeling and in expansion;
  - this removes the need for killed b_q altogether.

## 5. Cloud commands (not run locally: predicted over 1 GB or over 10 minutes)

Copy `ntrunc/t1` (including `knots4_cloud.txt` and `conway_rs.txt`) next to a krhom checkout. Change the path dependency in `t1/Cargo.toml` to that checkout. Then run `cargo build --release` and:

# (a) restricted lean BFS for (3,3,3,3), N = 6, and the two-tier bound 7 (types, time, memory, peel failures)

## 6. Open points

1. A paper that explicitly states the categorified kill criterion. The argument in §1(b) rests on (A1), the Hom formula at level N, which the engine already assumes.
2. The identification of krhom's singular KL basis with the U̇ / BLM canonical basis is assumed. It is consistent with the data, but not proved here.
3. Why the minimal-flow ladder's max label equals mu_1(p). It is observed for every type checked, but unproved.
4. On-demand killed-ladder characters X_q, needed to make the restricted basis work for 4 strands (§4).
5. Conway-knot truncated numbers at N = 6 (cloud (b)).
